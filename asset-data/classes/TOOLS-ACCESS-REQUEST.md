## OPTIONS

**O1 — Extend `lease-AS-HD-040-maker` to `tools/sprites-blender.py`.**
The remodel is a product behaviour change, which the ownership row already
assigns to maker; the path is unleased, so nothing is contested. This seat has
the measured target, the checker, and a drafted `build_rogue`.
*Cost:* a maker writing in a support-owned lane, against the lane's letter.

**O2 — Assign the work to an `it-support` seat.**
Follows `owner_role` literally. That seat inherits the measured envelope and the
checker and does not need to re-derive the art direction.
*Cost:* the lane exists to *restore the ability to work*, and its own note
excludes product behaviour changes. It would be doing maker work under a support
lease, and the round trip costs a re-seat.

**O3 — Split it: `build_rogue` first under O1, then rule on the three remodels.**
Landing `build_rogue` makes the approved look reproducible and proves the
procedural pipeline can reach it before anyone commits to three rewrites. The
larger question waits on evidence rather than on argument.
*Cost:* two decisions instead of one.

## RECOMMENDATION

**O3.** Today the approved look is 80,807 bytes nobody can regenerate; every
plan downstream assumes rogue is a stable target and it is not. `build_rogue` is
one function against a fixed, measurable gate — if the pipeline cannot reach the
approved look, that changes what the three remodels should even be, and it is
much cheaper to learn now.

O1 is the right long-run assignment if the ruling in §6 is that product
behaviour outranks the lane. O2 is defensible but pays a re-seat to move work to
a lane whose own note excludes it.
