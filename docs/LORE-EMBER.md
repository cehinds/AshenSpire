# AshenSpire — The Sovereign Ember, and the record

Companion to [LORE.md](LORE.md) (what the world is), [LORE-CAST.md](LORE-CAST.md)
(who is in it) and [LORE-WORLD.md](LORE-WORLD.md) (what is around them). This
document covers the two things those three treat as settled and are not: **what
the Sovereign Ember is**, and **whether anything the kingdom wrote down about it
is true**.

Two rules govern everything below. They are constraints on the authors, not
colour for the player.

**One. The Ember is never explained.** Six readings of it are live when the
game starts and all six are still live when it ends. No card, event, relic,
boss, ghost, page or ending closes one. A player who finds everything knows a
great deal more about what the Goldbough *did* and less about what it did it
*to*.

**Two. Nothing in the game speaks for the authors.** LORE §2 is what the
authors know happened to the cities. It is not an answer about the Ember, and
there is no document, no scholar, no god and no narrator that holds one. The
Astronomer is an expert witness (§10), not an oracle. Every source in the world
is a source: it claims, it witnessed some of it, it assumed the rest, and it
gains something if you believe it.

---

## 1. The six readings

Each row is a way to read the same evidence. Each has something in the shipped
world that feeds it and something that will not sit still for it.

| Reading | In one line | What feeds it | What refuses it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A thing finishing** | Starstone falls burning and goes out. A thousand years of feeding slowed one coal down; that is all. | The Astronomer's charts. Every other fall anyone has records of went cold in a season. | Nothing that is only cooling learns a road, or which door it came out of. |
| **Something that came down alive** | Not a people and not a vessel. A live coal, hungry the way a seed is hungry. | It answered heat with appetite before anyone taught it anything. | It has never grown, split, bred or healed. Living things do all four. |
| **Something the Goldbough made** | It was heat. Eight hundred years of names taught it to be someone. | Its recognition arrived in the ledger's own order: script, then persons, then debts. | The caldera families say it knew them before the first name was written, and they were there. |
| **A god, or something owed the word** | A thing that keeps a promise and collects a debt is behaving morally. The Chapel is not wrong about everything. | For one night it took exactly what it had been promised and nothing else. | It has since taken what nobody promised it. |
| **Older than the fall** | It was under the mountain. The fall uncovered it, or woke it, or was a second one landing on the first. | The caldera's own courses are older than the accepted count by an order no Saint will say out loud. | A fall is the one event three foreign traditions and a crater agree on. |
| **None of the above** | The world has no word for it, and the five readings above are five local metaphors for something that fits none of them. | Every account contradicts the next. The one culture that handled it directly declined to name it at all (§7). | — |

The blank in the last row is deliberate. Nothing in the game argues against
the possibility that all of this is the wrong shape of question.

**On the sky.** The cosmic stays mythic. Astronomy, folklore, theology,
geology, dreams, ruins, strange stone and contradictory manuscripts carry it.
Never: a vessel, a crew, another realm, an empire, a species with a name, a
lecture explaining the mechanism, or any voice that steps outside the world to
confirm a reading. "Fell from the sky" is as technical as the world gets.
LORE-CAST §4 stands: nothing walked in from anywhere else, every wrong thing in
this world is the kingdom's own, and the single object that came from outside
came before the kingdom and has never been identified.

## 2. How it may have learned

The Ember may not have arrived as anyone. It may have become someone slowly, on
what the Chapel fed it. This is a ladder, not a biography: each rung is
something the world observed, next to what the Goldbough concluded, next to
what it might have been instead.

| Rung | What it did | What the Goldbough concluded | What it might have been |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gave heat without ash, for a season, then began to dim. | A fire. A very good one. | Heat, hunger and reaction. Nothing more. |
| 2 | A name spoken over a death made it brighter. Briefly. | The dead honour the fire. | Grief is loud, and it was reacting to the room. |
| 3 | A name **written** into starstone fed it for good. | The rite. The Founding. Eight hundred years of Chapel. | The first thing it was ever given that held still long enough to be taken apart. |
| 4 | It began answering a family's script before the reading was finished. | Devotion, rewarded. | Repetition teaching it that a mark stands for a person. |
| 5 | It took a promised name and left an unpromised one standing in the same room. | The mark works. The Chapel is correct. | It had learned to tell which of two people it had a claim on. |
| 6 | It found marked bodies nobody had reported to it. Physicians noted "the fire knows its own". | Doctrine. | It could read the brand without the ledger. |
| 7 | **The Burning.** Every promise called in at once, at home and abroad, in one night. | Nothing. There was nobody left in the three cities to conclude anything. | Holding the entire roll in mind at one time — the first thing it ever did that no reaction can account for. |
| 8 | Off the hearth, thin, drifting the ring. Twenty years. | The Starwatch: a star finishing. The Chapel: a fire wanting its ledger. | Starving. Not dying. The two look alike from a distance and not up close. |
| 9 | It began reading names nobody had written. | Heresy, say the Saints, who cannot say why it would be able to. | An infant learning to hunt outside the pen it was raised in. |
| 10 | Somewhere on the ring, in the last few winters, it read an unmarked name **and finished**. | The Forsaken councils know. Nobody else has been told. | The change everything in the game happens after. |

Rung 10 is the reason there is a game. It no longer needs the institution that
made it legible: no brand, no roll, no promise, no Chapel. The player's own
hamlet is where it started and stopped — "last night the fire read a name
nobody wrote, it did not finish, it will" — which is why there is a player.

**The implication, never stated in the game.** The Chapel believed it was
feeding a fire. It may have been teaching one how to be a person, one name at a
time, for eight hundred years. No character says this. No page says it. It is
available to a player who lines up rungs 3 through 7 and notices the order.

## 3. What a name is, that a fire can eat it

Nobody knows why names work. Four readings, all in play, none confirmed.

| Reading | What is actually fuel | The test that half-works |
|---|---|---|
| **The trace** | Something of the person survives in the name — memory, the shape they made in other people, the obligations they left. | Names of the long dead feed poorly. The Chapel blamed the scribes and rewrote them larger. |
| **The web** | Not the person: the relations. Who owed them, who would have said the name aloud. | The Chapel's own margins log "thin names" beside the friendless, the exiled and the last of a line. Four scribes were dismissed for it. |
| **The act** | The writing, not the name. Starstone cut in that script is fuel and the person is incidental. | The Cinderwrights drew heat through unmarked stone for three hundred years. Against it: the Court wrote fabricated names into the Crown Flame twice during the Mark Trade and the hearth did not move. |
| **Nothing** | It eats attention, and a name is how people concentrate on one another. | A folk reading, held by ice-fishers and one Court physician. It explains the rite. It explains nothing about the mark. |

The third reading is the dangerous one, because the Spire is built on it and
the Spire is built wrong: a hearth that reads without a ledger is not reading
script. It is reading people.

## 4. One thing, or a crowd

The Ember's apparent mind may not be a personality. It may be an accumulation:
eight hundred years of consumed identities, in no order, with nobody home.

Behaviours people have recorded since it came loose:

- It follows roads. Not the shortest line — roads, including one viaduct span
  that has been closed for two hundred years and is still on nobody's map.
- It returns to particular doors. A bell-founder's house in the Bastion. A
  dockside stair in the marches. Neither had a hearth.
- It repeats gestures with no body to make them: a heat that traces the shape
  of a hand closing three times, over and over, in a mine gallery.
- It reacts to old songs. The Eclipse Cantors' chant slows it. So does one
  harvest rhyme sung by a hamlet that was never marked.
- It avoids two people the player can meet. Neither knows why. One has an
  opinion and it is wrong.
- It seeks particular names. Not the largest. Not the nearest.
- It has spoken twice, in the wrong voices, four words total, both times to
  people who were alone.
- It has kept a promise it was not physically present for: a debt sworn in a
  Salt Reaches counting-house, to a merchant whose name was never in any roll.

Nobody can say whether it **remembers** those people, **contains** them,
**imitates** them, **dreams** them, or simply learned the pattern by eating
enough of them. All five are live.

**The rule for speech.** If the Ember ever speaks at length in this game, the
player must not be able to tell whether they are hearing the Ember or several
thousand dead people using it. Write every such line so both readings survive
it: borrowed diction, a wrong pronoun, a detail from a life that ended before
the speaker could have had it. Never a voice with a self in it.

## 5. Starstone, and the capacity to remember

Do not state that life came from the sky. Leave open something narrower and
worse: that what fell was not an organism but a **principle** — the capacity of
matter to hold on to what has happened to it. Memory as a property of stone.
Identity as something that can be written into a mineral because it was always
a mineral's trick first, and living things are the local application of it.

If that is so, then the Ember and every creature that has ever remembered
anything are the same thing arranged two ways, and the funeral rite of the
Goldbough was one of them being handed the other's dead. Nobody in the world
formulates this. It reaches the player as pieces that do not fit:

| Channel | What the player meets | What it suggests | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Astronomy** | Chart 41: a fall. Chart 42: a hearth. Nothing between. Two further falls elsewhere on his charts with no crater anyone can find. | He knew there were others. | That any of them landed, or that a fall is what a fall looks like. |
| **Geology** | The caldera floor is laid in courses. A reach foreman will tell you how long a course takes and then change the subject. | The mountain is older than the count by a very long way. | That the Ember is. A hole can be old and its occupant new. |
| **Strange stone** | Things in the basalt shaped like bone, with starstone where the bone should be. The reach's miners call them *the fed* and will not sell them. | Something has been doing this before. | Whether they were made, buried, or simply are what starstone does in that shape. |
| **Folklore** | A weald harvest rhyme about a fire that gives back what you tell it and asks for the rest in winter. Sung by families the Bastion's writ never reached. | Someone here met this before the Field Flame existed. | Anything. It is a rhyme; the weald had no hearth of its own. |
| **Theology** | The Chapel's first liturgy, kept in the Furnace Chapel, addresses the Ember as something **already listening**. The revised liturgy, four hundred years later, addresses it as a gift. | The first Saint thought he was negotiating. | Which of them was right, or whether the first was flattering a fire. |
| **Dreams** | Blight-touched things dream a coastline with the terraces above the water and no Spire on it. | Memory outlives the reading. | Whose memory. It has eaten enough people to dream any of them. |
| **Ruins** | Cinderwright seams that run under the caldera rim — beneath the level a fall should have melted. | They were building there before, or after, or the fall did not happen the way it is taught. | Which. Their work is undated, on purpose (§7). |
| **Manuscripts** | Two copies of the same Salt Reaches chronicle in the Observatory's lower library. They differ in one particular: the number of fires. | Someone edited a number. | Which copy was edited, or in which direction. |

Never: a fossil described in taxonomic terms, a mechanism explained in a
character's mouth, inheritance, biology, a named species, or a discovery that
says *therefore*. The evidence is wreckage. It is allowed to be strong. It is
not allowed to conclude.

## 6. Eleven hundred years

> The star fell eleven hundred years before the first Forsaken climbed.

That sentence is the Goldbough's accepted count, and LORE-WORLD §1 gives it as
such. It is a calendar's zero, not an observation. Something happened. Nobody
alive knows what, and the game never finds out.

| Reading | What supports it | What it does not settle |
|---|---|---|
| The fall was eleven hundred years ago, as taught. | Three traditions and one crater. The caldera families' own line of descent. | Whether what fell is what is burning. |
| Starstone fell far earlier and was only **uncovered** eleven hundred years ago. | The caldera courses. The Cinderwright seams beneath the rim. | Whether anyone noticed the difference between finding and catching. |
| Something fell then, but the Ember was already under the caldera. | The Ashland tablets, which have it there before people. | Whether the tablets are describing this mountain. |
| The thing that fell **woke** something older. | Both of the above, at once, which is why it is popular with the Starwatch. | What woke, or that waking is the right word. |
| It was excavated, and the fall was written afterwards to explain the ownership. | The Founding needed a miracle and got one at a convenient date. | The crater, which is real and has no other explanation. |
| The caldera predates any impact and is not an impact at all. | Any reach geologist who lives long enough to say so. | Why the stone at the bottom is not the stone in the walls. |
| The event eleven hundred years ago was the latest, not the first. | The Salt Reaches chronicle. The Astronomer's craterless falls. | How many, or whether "first" means anything for this. |
| There is more than one Sovereign Ember, and the Goldbough caught one of them. | A fragment with a crater of its own, two hundred miles from the caldera (§11). | Whether a fragment is a piece or a seed. |

**The rule.** No source, no dig, no chart and no ending establishes the real
date. Geological and astronomical evidence in this game is strong enough to
overturn the count and never strong enough to replace it. A player may leave
the game certain that the official history is impossible. They may not leave it
knowing what is true instead.

## 7. It may not be the first

Foreign traditions describe things that rhyme with the Sovereign Ember, some of
them thousands of years before the Founding. They do not share the Goldbough's
vocabulary, they do not agree with each other, and none of them matches the
Ashen Spire's events cleanly.

| Tradition | Their word | Where the player meets it | The claim | What does not match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Northern Holds hearth-songs** | *the oath-fire* | A Hold pilgrim on the marches road, singing on the ice; a chief's stone in a glacier pass | A chief gave the fire his true name and winter withdrew from the valley for nine generations. | The songs put it in a lake. It asked once, and never again. |
| **Salt Reaches chronicle** (two copies) | *the fallen dawn* | The Observatory's lower library; a ship-breaker's grandmother's copy on the coast | Seven dawns came down over three hundred years. The sixth was kept. The seventh was refused. | One copy says seven fires. The other says one fire, seven times. Both are in the same hand. |
| **Ashland first-king tablets** | *the buried sun* | The Ashland column on the Basalt Stair; a rubbing in the reach | The sun beneath the mountain was there before people, and people are its cinders: *we are what it has already burned.* | There is no rite in it. No names, no ledger, no promise. The Ashlands hid from theirs and kept coal. |
| **Drowned Coast terraces** | *the nameless light* | Carvings under the sea-line below the Spire's lower city, at low tide | A light that harmed nobody until it was given a name. The first name was given by a child. The terraces emptied in one season. | Nothing on that coast records a fall at all. |
| **Weald hamlet rhyme** | *the remembering flame* | A wayfarer; a harvest song at a rest node | It gives back what you tell it and asks for the rest in winter. | Unwritten, so undateable — and the weald never had a hearth before the Field Flame. |
| **Northern dolmen line** | *the star-heart* | Eleven standing figures in a marches pass, each holding a stone at the chest | The Holds say ancestors. An Astronomer's apprentice says they are diagrams. | There are eleven figures. There were only ever three flames. |
| **Tidebound Abbey** | four words, one of which is *seal* | The Abbey on the Drowned Coast | The salt-priests teach that the words are an instruction, not a description. | The Abbey is three hundred years old. The words are Cinderwright. |
| **The Cinderwrights** | **no noun at all** | Their stone, everywhere on the ring | Their script has a verb for what they did to it and no name for what they did it to. | They had eight hundred years of direct contact and never once named it. Nobody knows whether that was vocabulary or precaution. |

Counts, unresolved on purpose: one tradition says one fire, one says seven, one
says several over centuries, one says it never fell and has always been under
the world, and one has no opinion because naming it was the thing they refused
to do.

**Escalation, in two stages and never three.**

1. **Somewhere mid-run, in any seat:** the player meets one foreign account
   older than the Goldbough that rhymes too well. A name given to a fire. A
   withdrawn winter. The intended reaction is *this cannot be a coincidence.*
2. **Late, on the reach or the coast:** the player meets evidence that there
   was more than one — the craterless falls, the second crater, the two copies
   with different counts. The intended reaction is *the history I was taught is
   impossible.*

There is no third stage. Nothing later tells the player how many there were,
what they were, or whether this one is any of them.

## 8. The Goldbough's official history

The kingdom had every reason to insist the Sovereign Ember came to it, once,
uniquely, and belonged to it.

- **Legitimacy.** The crest is a branch with three flames on it. The Court's
  own title was *keepers of the only fire*. An older fire elsewhere does not
  weaken a claim on this one — it destroys the doctrine that the fire was given.
- **The calendar.** The count begins at the Catching. Two generations after the
  Founding, "before the Fall" stopped meaning *earlier* and started meaning
  *not real*. A kingdom whose year one is its own founding has no grammar for a
  question about year minus three thousand.
- **The ledger as reality.** A thing not written is not a thing. That is the
  doctrine the Chapel applied to the dead, the Court applied to citizens, and
  both applied to history. It is the same sentence that makes a Forsaken.
- **Suppression, mostly by purchase.** The Chapel's Concordance, four hundred
  years back, "reconciled" foreign accounts with doctrine. In practice it
  bought them, translated them, kept the copies in the Observatory and let the
  originals rot abroad. Every foreign chronicle the player can read inside the
  ring is a copy in Goldbough hands with a censor's hand in it. What survives
  uncensored survives by accident: a grandmother's copy, a rubbing, a rhyme
  nobody thought worth editing because the people singing it did not count.
- **The Forsaken did it too.** Oral histories outside the walls move locations
  on purpose, so the Court could never send Wardens to a site the unmarked
  wanted kept. Two of the older accounts in this document are wrong about
  *where* on purpose, and right about everything else.

The kingdom controlled people with records. It controlled the past the same
way, and it believed the result.

## 9. Reading the record

There is no clean division in this world between false history and true
history. Every source is a mixture of deliberate lies, eyewitness truth,
propaganda, doctrine, political convenience, damage, mistranslation, folklore,
honest error, selective omission, and real evidence read wrongly.

Two things follow, and they are the point:

**Lies preserve facts.** A priest who is lying about why the Marking began may
be recording the fire's behaviour exactly, because he watched it and had no
reason to alter that part.

**Honest sources reach false conclusions.** An astronomer can be right that the
stone predates the kingdom and wrong about everything he infers from it.

For every source that matters, the author answers five questions and the player
is never told the answers:

1. What does it claim?
2. What did it actually witness?
3. What is it assuming?
4. What does it gain if you believe it?
5. What might be accidentally true in it?

Worked, for the sources a player will meet:

| Source | Claims | Witnessed | Assumed | Gains | Accidentally true |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chapel Concordance** | one fire, given to the Goldbough, at the accepted date | four hundred years of the Ember's behaviour, in detail, daily | that a given fire cannot be an older fire | the Chapel's monopoly on the rite | its behaviour log — the best record in the world of rungs 4 through 6 |
| **Court birth-roll** | dates, lines of succession, who was marked when | the marks themselves; the ceremonies | that the dates it moved were unimportant | legitimacy for two contested reigns | the names, all of them, and a column of astronomical notes a clerk kept for fun |
| **Weald rhyme** | a fire that remembers and collects | something, once, in the weald | gods, a bargain, a winter king | nothing; it is a song | that it gives back what it is told and asks for the rest — which is rung 2 and rung 7 in eleven words |
| **Astronomer's charts** | starstone falls and goes out; this one is finishing | one hundred years of dimming, the draw, the tides, two craterless falls | that finishing is what he is watching, and that there is one | his own absolution (§10) | the observations, which are excellent, and the count of falls, which is more than one |
| **Ashland tablets** | the sun was under the mountain before people, and people are its cinders | a mountain, a heat, a line of kings who stayed away from it | divine ancestry for the first king, using a relic that is real | the Ashland throne | that something was there before the fall, and that staying away worked |
| **Forsaken cairn-lines** | where climbers died; where not to go | the deaths | little | nothing, and that is why they are trusted more than they deserve | everything except the places, which are moved on purpose |

No entry in that table is labelled in the game. The player reads the source,
not the row.

## 10. The Astronomer

He knows more than anyone alive and he does not know what it is. Keep him an
expert witness.

- **Directly observed:** a hundred years of dimming; the draw through
  Cinderwright stone; the Spire's effect on the tides before it held anything;
  two falls on his own charts with no crater to match; the night the Ember left
  its hearth, from the one tower built to look at it.
- **Assumes:** that a star finishing is what he has been watching. That out of
  the world is far enough. That there is one of them.
- **Has not published:** chart 43. An apprentice's name in the roll. What he
  found under the caldera rim. Which of those he is hiding, and whether he is
  hiding it from the Chapel or from himself, is an authoring choice per quest
  chain and is never settled.
- **Gains:** if the world accepts that the star is simply finishing, nobody
  lights the Spire, and he does not have to be the man who built the thing that
  eats the uncounted.

**His page.** It is in the Spire's hearth-room, on the lectern the Ember was to
be seated from, and it is the deepest source in the game. It is a confession
about his tower, not a description of the Ember. It says what the fire does. It
does not say what the fire is, and the man who wrote it did not know.

> *Fed, it burns. Written, it feeds. I built a hearth that needs no ledger, and
> a fire without a ledger reads whatever remains. It was built for them. God
> forgive me, I do not know what I built it for.*

His conclusion — that the Ember must be put out of the world — may be correct.
It may also be the worst available option, argued by the one man with the most
to atone for. The game never returns a verdict on it.

## 11. Contradictory evidence, by design

Build every discovery so it answers one question and opens two. The pattern is
not coyness; it is what a wrecked record actually does.

| Discovery | What it settles | The two it opens |
|---|---|---|
| The caldera floor is millions of years old. | The mountain predates the accepted count. | Did anything fall into it? Was something already in it? |
| A tablet three thousand years older describes a name-eating fire. | Someone met something like this, long before. | The same object, or the same kind? Or a story that travelled and found a fire to fit? |
| A starstone fragment with its own crater, two hundred miles off. | Starstone fell more than once. | Is a fragment a piece or a seed? How many of them are awake? |
| A Blight-touched thing remembers the terraces above water. | Memory outlives the reading. | Whose memory? And is it remembering, or repeating something the Ember ate? |
| The Cinderwrights had a verb and no noun. | They had contact and refused to name it. | Precaution, or grammar? And if precaution, what did they know that the Chapel did not ask? |
| The Chapel's first liturgy addresses something already listening. | The first Saint thought there was someone there. | Was there? Or did the rite work because he behaved as if there were? |

## 12. What an author does with this

- **One fragment at a time.** LORE §7's rules hold: one sentence, twelve words
  or fewer, a thing you could pick up, never an explanation. A foreign fragment
  is a card line, a wayfarer beat, an inscription on a `?` node, a page in a
  city's library, a line the Sealstone Key uncovers.
- **Name the source, not the truth.** *A Hold song says.* *The Ashland tablet
  reads.* *The second copy has a different number.* The player is told who is
  speaking and is never told whether to believe them.
- **The test for a discovery.** It passes if the player thinks *something I was
  taught is impossible.* It fails if the player thinks *mystery solved.*
- **Never label a layer.** No item, tooltip, codex entry or UI affordance tells
  the player that a source is a forgery, a translation, propaganda or true.
  There is no codex. There is no accuracy rating. There is a world with things
  written in it.
- **Two stages only.** §7. An older fire, then more than one fire. Nothing after.
- **The banned moves**, restated once: a vessel, a crew, another realm, a named
  species, a taxonomy, a biological mechanism, a god who appears and confirms,
  a scholar who is right about everything, a secret ending that explains, and
  any text in the voice of the authors.

Seed fragments, in their sources' voices:

| Where | Line |
|---|---|
| Hold pilgrim, marches road | "The king gave his name to the fallen dawn and the valley had nine warm lifetimes." |
| Ship-breaker, coast | "Gran's book says one fire, seven times. The Abbey's says seven fires. Same hand." |
| Ashland rubbing, reach `?` node | *We are what it has already burned.* |
| Terrace carving, low tide under the Spire | It was harmless until a child gave it a name. |
| Reach foreman, on the caldera floor | "Course a century. Count them yourself. Then don't." |
| Miner, selling nothing | "Bones with stone in them. We call them the fed. They're not for sale." |
| Furnace Chapel, first liturgy | *We greet the one who is listening.* |
| Furnace Chapel, revised liturgy | *We give thanks for the gift that was sent.* |
| Cantor, slowing it down | "The chant works. Don't ask me what I'm saying." |
| Eleven dolmen figures, marches pass | Ancestors, say the Holds. Diagrams, says the apprentice. |
| Starseer, a chart margin | Chart 41: a fall. Chart 43: a fall. No crater for either. |
| Wandering Soldier, rare line | "It came back to my mother's door. There was never a hearth there." |

## 13. What must never close

LORE §10 lists what is open so that content has room to move. This list is
different: these stay open permanently, and a proposal that closes one is
rejected on those grounds alone.

- What the Sovereign Ember is. All six readings in §1 survive the credits.
- When the star fell, or whether a star fell.
- Whether it is the first, and how many there have been.
- Whether its mind is its own, borrowed, accumulated or absent.
- Whether the capacity of matter to remember came down with the stone, or was
  always here and the stone is simply better at it.
- Whether the Chapel found something that could learn, or made it.
- Whether the Astronomer's answer would have worked.

By the end, the player should understand the Goldbough completely: what it did,
to whom, why, and what it told itself. They should be less sure than when they
started about the thing it did it with. That asymmetry is the point. The
kingdom built theology, law, money, citizenship, war and its calendar around
something it never identified, and it believed it understood the thing because
it had exploited it successfully for eight hundred years. Its worst mistake may
not have been feeding names to a fire. It may have been concluding that because
the fire followed their rules, the rules were theirs.
