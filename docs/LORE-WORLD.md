# AshenSpire — The world

Companion to [LORE.md](LORE.md) (what the world is), [LORE-CAST.md](LORE-CAST.md)
(who is in it) and [LORE-EMBER.md](LORE-EMBER.md) (what is not known and stays
that way). This is everything around them: history, magic, war, faith,
equipment, neighbours, peoples, ecology, factions, and what everyone wants.
Grounded in the game's own vocabulary — the five damage schools (`physical`,
`magic`, `arcane`, `holy`, `fire`), the statuses (Bleed, Frost, Insanity, Blight,
Burn, Starstone charge, Stagger), the Crimson and Azure flasks, the kits (Iron
Vanguard, Ash Focus, Bone Rite), and the relics and events that already ship.

The rule stands: none of this is told as history in the game. It is told as
weather, stonework, a coin, a line.

---

## 1. History — six ages and one night

Dates in this table are the **Goldbough's accepted count**: its calendar begins
at the Catching, and every figure below is a number the kingdom wrote about
itself. Something happened eleven hundred years ago. What it was, whether a
star was involved, and whether it was the first time are open permanently —
[LORE-EMBER.md](LORE-EMBER.md) §6 and §7 hold the readings and the rule that
none of them ever wins. Read the table as doctrine with good bookkeeping.

| Age | When (before the Burning) | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **The Fall** | ~1,100 years, by the count | A star fell into the caldera of what is now the Cinder Reach — so the kingdom teaches. Starstone, burning. The people who lived around the caldera caught it before it went out. The crater is real. The caldera is much older than the count, and the courses under it are older than that. |
| **The Cinderwrights** | 1,100 – 800 years | The catchers learned what starstone answers to — rhythm, draw, seal — and built with it: the viaduct ring, the sealstone doors, the astronomical stone the Observatory stands on, the standing stones that hum. They never wrote a name into the Ember. They **sealed** it, and drew its heat through stone. Their script has a verb for what they did to it and no noun for the thing itself, and some of their seams run under the caldera rim, below where a fall should have melted them. |
| **The Founding of the Goldbough** | ~800 years | A caldera priest — the first Saint — discovered that a name spoken into the Ember at death made it burn brighter, and that a name *written* fed it for good. The Cinderwrights refused. They were absorbed in two generations: their ring inherited, their script kept only by cantors and astronomers, their name forgotten. Three hearths lit from the one Ember. The kingdom took the gilded branch as its crest. |
| **The Marking** | ~400 years | The kingdom outgrew its dead. The Saints began marking the living at birth: a promise of the name later, a citizen's right to warmth now. Everyone inside the walls, marked. Everyone outside, not. The Forsaken are born here as a category. |
| **The Spring Wars** | ~300 – 150 years | The Field Flame turned the seasons for whoever paid. The kingdom never fielded an army against a neighbour; it withheld a spring. The Northern Holds, the Salt Reaches and the Ashland Kings all bought their years from the Bastion. Tribute was called *the hour*. |
| **The Mark Trade** | last 100 years | The Court sold marks abroad: foreign nobles promised to the fire for a fee, warm anywhere on the ring. The roll of promised names grew faster than any city's births. The Chapel was not told. |
| **The Spire** | last 20 years | The Ember dimming past what the ledger could feed. The Astronomer's fourth hearth, built to burn without written names. Finished the spring before the end. Never lit. |
| **The Burning** | one night, 20 years ago | The Ember called every promise in at once, from the inside, at home and abroad. The light collapsed inward. Three cities burned toward their towers. The Ember left its hearth. |
| **The Long Winter** | now | The seasons stopped where they stood. The Blight collects slowly. The unmarked inherit a ring they were never allowed on. The Ember drifts toward the Spire, and has begun to read names nobody wrote. |

**What came before the kingdom** was better at this than the kingdom was. The
Cinderwrights drew heat without spending anyone. The Founding was a shortcut
that worked for eight hundred years and then took the whole bill at once.

**What came before that** is a fog the Goldbough had no reason to clear. Two
generations after the Founding, *before the Fall* stopped meaning *earlier* in
court usage and started meaning *not real*. Foreign traditions put fires like
this one thousands of years earlier, in words the ring does not use — an
oath-fire, a fallen dawn, a buried sun, a nameless light — and contradict each
other about how many there were. The Chapel's Concordance bought and
translated most of them four hundred years ago. What survived uncensored
survived by accident.

## 2. Magic — five schools, one substance

Everything that is not steel comes from starstone, and starstone answers to
three things: **rhythm** (the Starseer's sequence), **writing** (the Chapel's
ledger), and **draw** (the Cinderwrights' stone and the Spire). Nobody knows
why any of the three works. The most common learned guess — held by one
Astronomer's apprentice and no institution — is that starstone holds on to what
happens to it, and that a name written into it is the only thing anyone has
ever handed it that was shaped to be held on to. Where that property comes from,
and whether living things have it for the same reason, is open and stays open
([LORE-EMBER.md](LORE-EMBER.md) §3, §5). The five damage
schools are five relationships with it:

| School | Source | Who | Status it leaves | What it is, in-world |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **physical** | steel, blood, weight | Reaver, Rogue, every soldier | Bleed, Stagger | the unwritten ledger: blood is a name nobody has to write down, and a thing with a cinder in it still has blood to lose. Stagger is the Wardens' *hold* broken. |
| **magic** | starstone, cut and charged | Starseer | Frost, Starstone charge | starstone is cold until it is asked in the right order. Frost is starstone remembering the sky. Frost-exposed things take starstone harder because they have been reminded what they are made of. |
| **arcane** | Cinderwright script, sealstone, the heavens | the Astronomer, Mirror Scribes, Eclipse Cantors, the Sealstone Key | Insanity, Madness | reading what was sealed rather than written. The script makes teeth ache because it is meant to be *drawn through*, not read; a mind that reads it holds a draw it cannot release. |
| **holy** | gold, the ledger, the mark | Herald, Saints, Valkyries | Blight (crimsonBlight), Regen | the Chapel's writing turned to power. Healing is a name fed early; rot is the same act with the sign reversed. The Herald's Blight is holy magic with no ledger to bill. |
| **fire** | ember, cinders | Furnace Saint, Cinderbear, Gorefire | Burn | the Ember's own heat. Every fire attack is a cinder spent. Gorefire Stance is a Forsaken carrying a flame the only way one can: in the blade, burning the hand. |

**Flasks** are the two substances a body can hold: **Crimson** (blood, the
unwritten ledger, heals) and **Azure** (cut starstone in suspension, restores
the draw). The Wandering Physician trades in both and asks a little flesh.

**Marks and writing.** A mark is a promise; writing is payment. A written name
keeps the Ember lit and is gone. A marked, unwritten name is fuel the fire has
a claim on and has not collected — which is what the Blight is collecting.

**Sealing and draw** are the old way and the new heresy: the Cinderwrights
sealed the Ember and drew heat through stone; the Spire copies the draw at a
scale nobody tested, and a draw with no seal reads whatever it can reach.

**Relics** are cinders in objects: a hearth-key holds a seat's largest; the
Ember Fragment, the Kindling Charm and the Cinder Pouch are small ones. The
Sealstone Key is Cinderwright work and reads their marks on the map, which is
why it shows what a `?` node is: the ring was always labelled, in a script.

## 3. War and combat

**Before.** The kingdom had three armed orders and never one army:

| Order | Seat | Arms | Doctrine | What it became |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wardens** | Bastion | halberd, kite and tower shields, the bell | *the hold*: a line does not break, it staggers and re-forms; signals by bell-roll | Wandering Soldiers, Husk Brutes, the Fell Warden, the Thorn Matriarch |
| **Knights of the Court** | Citadel | rapier, mirror plate, duel law | *the oath*: single combat as legal process; a knight's word binding on the fire | Gilded Knights, Duelists, Living Armor, the Glass Regent |
| **Censer-bearers** | Observatory / Chapel | censer, lance, gilt | *the writing*: the Chapel's soldiers were written as winged before they died, so that they would be | Valkyrie Shades, the Blighted Valkyrie, Eclipse Cantors |
| **Wyrm Aspirants** | the reach | halberd, consecration | *the sleep*: keep the dragon under by feeding the caldera the mine's own cinders | Wyrm Aspirants, Wyrm Lords, still consecrating nothing |

The Forsaken had no order. Hamlet militias, ship-breaker gangs, mine-camp
foremen, and sellswords the cities hired and never marked (the Reaver).

**How fighting works, in-world.** Poise is the Warden hold: break it and the
thing staggers and can be finished. Bleed is blood as the ledger the fire
cannot read. Frost is starstone reminded of the sky. Blight is the fire's own
collection, turned. Stances are drill: the Wardens taught two. Weapon arts are
the orders' techniques mounted on a blade, which is why a card can be *mounted*
at the Smith.

**Foreign wars** were seasons, not battles. The Spring Wars were fought by the
Bastion withholding the hour; a Hold that fell behind got a late spring and
lost a harvest. The one real war — **the Salt War**, ~200 years ago — was the
Salt Reaches refusing tribute and the kingdom's answer being three winters in a
row; the Reaches' poor fled to the coast and became the ship-breakers. The
Ashland Kings were never fought: they sold ember-ore and the kingdom needed it.

## 4. Faiths

| Faith | Who | Creed | Rite | After the Burning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Writing** | the Chapel, the Saints, crown-born | the dead keep the living warm; a name written is a name kept | the funeral: your name into the Crown Flame | the Furnace Saint feeds an empty hearth; the Herald is its heresy-in-person |
| **The Hour** | the Wardens, field-born | the bell tells the season; the land answers the flame | dawn peal, harvest peal, the muster peal | the Bell Keeper rings for a dawn that does not come; every marked thing in the weald walks toward the sound |
| **The Oath** | the Court, court-born | the flame witnesses; a sworn word is a mark on the mark | swearing before the throne | the Stitched King's Thousand Hands: every oath-taker still sewn to his word |
| **The Sleep** | the Wyrm Aspirants, the reach | the wyrm dreams the heat; keep it dreaming | consecration; cinders into the caldera | Aspirants consecrate nothing; the dragon stirs |
| **The Starwatch** | the Astronomer and apprentices | not a faith, a discipline: the star is finishing; measure it | the eclipse vigil | the Hollow Astronomer calls the heavens down; the Oracle keeps the fourth question |
| **The Feral Ember** | the Blight-Priest's cult, marched and unmarked alike | *the Blight gives*: burning is the sacrament, finish it gladly | the shrine of antlers, wax and old blood | growing. The only faith recruiting. |
| **The Salt** | the coast, the Tidebound Abbey, the ship-breakers | the sea takes and gives; nothing written survives water | a name spoken over the side, never written | the sea took the lower city; the Abbey watches the Spire and prays it stays dark |
| **The Cairn** | the Forsaken | no names; a sword stands for you | broken swords in a mound | the Grave of the Nameless; the Keeper |

**The heresy charge** is the world's oldest weapon: the Chapel called the
Cinderwrights heretics for sealing, the Spire heresy for drawing, the Herald
heresy for surviving. Every faith above except the Cairn has burned someone
for a doctrine, and the Cairn only because it has no one to burn.

## 5. Equipment — five materials

| Material | Where | Who works it | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Steel** | Bastion forges, weald iron | Wardens; the Iron Vanguard kit; Guardian, Bastion and Spiked Reprisal shields | the unmarked's metal: it holds, it does not burn, it asks nothing |
| **Gold** | Court gilt, Chapel leaf | Knights, Saints; Goldleaf Charm, Gilded Tear, Radiant Aegis | holy conduit; gold carries writing. A gilded blade is a signed one. |
| **Ash** | reach basalt, obsidian, ember-tempered | miners, Aspirants; the Ash Focus kit, Ashheart claws | steel that has been through the fire once and will hold a cinder |
| **Blood** | rites | the Herald's Bone Rite kit, Sacrificial Knife, Bloodstained Chalice, Crimson Covenant | the unwritten ledger made into a tool; every blood item is a promise made to no fire |
| **Starstone** | Observatory-cut, rare | Starseer; the Starstone Focus kit, Starstone Shard, Kris, Azure Sigil | the Ember's own substance, cold, asked in order |

**Keepsakes** (Old Cinder, Traveler's Flask, Whetstone Memory) are what a
Forsaken carries from outside: a coal from a home hearth that was never the
kingdom's, a flask, a stone that remembers an edge. **Smithing** is
Cinderwright craft half-remembered: the Sleeping Smith is a mine-camp Forsaken
who learned to work ash from the sealstone seams. He can reforge a hearth-key
into a weapon (the Fell Warden Brand is one), which means that seat's flame
cannot be relit this run.

## 6. Neighbours, peoples, and the geopolitical situation

One species. Many peoples. The kingdom's "races" were legal, not biological,
and the world's cruelty runs along that line.

### Inside the ring (the Goldbough)

| People | Seat | Rank among the marked | Trade | Grudge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Crown-born** | Observatory / Chapel | highest: written first, warmest | the ledger, ember, doctrine | the Court sold marks behind their backs |
| **Court-born** | Citadel | second: the law and the money | gilt, law, the mark trade | the Chapel's Decree marked their debtors without asking |
| **Field-born** | Bastion | third: the land and the levy | harvest, steel, the hour | both of the above lived on their seasons |
| **The reach's miners** | Cinder Reach | marked late, ranked lowest of the marked | ember-ore, cinders, ash steel | fed a star and a dragon and were called dirty for it |
| **The Forsaken** | outside every wall | unmarked; not ranked; not counted | fish, salt, lichen, carrion, sellsword work, the merchant's cart | everything |

Among the Forsaken themselves: **weald hamlets** (farmers the Bastion's writ
never reached), **ice-fishers** (Northern Hold refugees from a withheld
spring), **mine camps** (the reach's unmarked labour), **ship-breakers** (the
Salt War's exiles on the coast). They do not like each other. A hamlet thinks a
ship-breaker is a foreigner; a ship-breaker thinks a hamlet had it soft.

### Beyond the ring

| Neighbour | Where | Before | What they wanted | After | What they want now |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Northern Holds** | past the marches' ice | clans paying *the hour* for their springs; sent sons to be marked | a reliable year; marks for their chiefs | frozen in the winter they were being punished with; their marked chiefs burned in the night | warmth. Their pilgrims walk in across the ice and become Ember-Starved. |
| **The Salt Reaches** | across the sea | city-states, traders, the only fleet; refused tribute once and lost three winters | the ring's warmth by sea; later, marks for their merchant houses | ships stopped coming when the Spire's draw turned the tides; their marked houses burned | to know why. No ship has come in twenty years. The Abbey watches the horizon for the first. |
| **The Ashland Kings** | past the reach, over the caldera rim | rival miners; sold ember-ore; kept small false hearths of coal | ember-ore prices; the reach's mines | the only warm people left, on coal; never marked, never burned | the ring. An Ashland column is on the Basalt Stair now, with coal and steel, to take a hearth they will not know how to light. |
| **The Cinderwrights** | nowhere; everywhere | the builders | to seal, never to spend | gone eight hundred years | nothing. But their doors still answer a key, and someone still chants in their tongue. |

Each of these peoples carries an older account of a fire like the ring's, in
its own vocabulary, and none of the accounts agree — the Holds sing one fire
that asked once, the Salt Reaches copied a chronicle that says seven (or one,
seven times, depending on the copy), the Ashland tablets put a sun under the
mountain before there were people at all. The table of traditions, what each
claims and what each fails to match is in [LORE-EMBER.md](LORE-EMBER.md) §7.

**The geopolitical situation, in one line:** three empty warm cities, one
hearth that must not light, an uncounted people who alone can walk between,
and every neighbour who ever bought a spring now walking toward the light or
marching toward the stair.

## 7. Hidden high culture — the Cinderwrights

Rare to spot, and only by architecture:

- **Stone with no mortar and no tool marks**, seams you cannot get a knife
  into: the viaduct piers, the Observatory's lower six courses, the
  Bellfoundry's foundation, the Ossuary Organ Hall's pipes (the Court built an
  organ into something that was already a resonator).
- **Script that makes your teeth ache**: the Ancient Cinder Stone on the road;
  the Mirror Scribes' silver script is a Court copy of it that only half works.
- **Doors that answer a sealstone**: the Sealstone Key opens what the kingdom
  could only wall up. The `?` nodes it reveals are the ring's original labels.
- **The ring itself is a machine.** It was built to carry heat around the
  three hearths through stone. That is why the Ember can *drift* on it, and
  why the fourth spur, unjoined, is a draw with no return.
- **Who still knows:** the Eclipse Cantors chant it without understanding; the
  Astronomer's apprentices read it (the Starseer can); the Oracle, his first
  apprentice, keeps the fourth question; the salt-priests of the Tidebound
  Abbey have four words of it, one of which is *seal*.

They were not another race. They were the people who caught the star and were
absorbed by the people who learned to feed it. Their technology was higher
because it did not spend anyone, and it was lost because it was slower.

## 8. The food chain — everything eats warmth now

**Before:** a scheduled ecology. Marked herds (a warm herd), kennel hounds,
Warden-kept bears, moths bred beside hearths for their dust, mine-vermin, the
sea's fish on the coast. At the top, the wyrm, asleep, fed cinders.

**After:** the seasons stopped, so the ecology stopped where it stood and then
went wrong in place.

| Tier | Who | Eats |
|---|---|---|
| Top | **the Ashheart Dragon** | the deep heat; waking |
| | **the Ember** | names — written, and now unwritten |
| | **the corrupted** | nothing. They do not eat; they *drain* (Grave Wisp, Ember-Starved Pilgrim). A corrupted thing near a warm thing takes its warmth. |
| Middle | **the wild gone wrong**: Blight Hounds feral, Cinderbears, Handspiders and the Cinder Mantis (a weald stuck in spring is an insect paradise), Stitch Crabs on the frozen river, Lantern Moths | each other; the Forsaken's stock; ember dust |
| | **carrion-eaters** (Carrion Talon, Carrion Morsel, the Grave of Ships' gulls) | the only tier that is thriving |
| Bottom | **the unmarked** | ice-fish through a lake that does not thaw; mine-camp lichen; salt and what the sea gives back; the merchant's tinned goods at his prices; and, where a Physician asks for a little flesh, each other |
| | **the Forsaken's stock**: goats, a few unmarked herds the fire never claimed | the stalled spring's grass, endlessly |

Heat is the currency of the chain the way it is the currency of the run: a
cinder in a pouch is the only warm thing a Forsaken owns, and every creature on
the ring can smell it.

## 10. Prejudice, betrayal, conspiracy, and hope

**Prejudice.** Marked over unmarked is the kingdom's racism, and it did not
die with the kingdom: a corrupted knight will still not speak to a Forsaken.
Among the marked: crown over court over field over miner. Among the Forsaken:
hamlet over fisher over camp over ship-breaker. Born-marked over bought-marked.
And the newest one, among the living: **finish them** versus **leave them** —
whether the corrupted are citizens to be released or monsters to be avoided,
argued in every hamlet council and settled, badly, by whoever climbs.

**Betrayals.** The Founding betrayed the Cinderwrights. The Marking betrayed
the unborn. The Mark Trade betrayed the Chapel; the Decree betrayed the Court's
debtors; both betrayed the foreign marked, who burned for a fee. The Spire
betrayed the unwritten before it was lit. The Aspirants fed the dragon the
mine's own cinders, which were the miners' own dead. The Bell Keeper's peal
calls every marked thing in the weald to the fire, and he knows it.

**Conspiracies still running.** The Court's ledger of sold marks exists and
the Oracle knows where. The Sleeping Smith reforges hearth-keys and does not
say the flame then cannot be lit. The merchant's three. The Tidebound Abbey
has a boat. The Ashland column has a Forsaken guide who took coal for the map.

**Secrets the player can find.** Which three the merchant keeps warm. The
Astronomer's page. What the Herald's mark said. Where the ledger of sold marks
is. That the Keeper burned first. That the ring was built by someone else.

**Hope.** A season can be turned; the mechanism still exists. The Keeper did
not finish burning, which means the fire *can* fail. Nobody has tried a name
given freely. Pilgrims still walk in from outside, which means the outside
still looks toward the ring instead of away. And the Forsaken are, for the
first time in eight hundred years, the only people who can go inside.

## 11. What was coming, what is coming

- **Before the Burning** the kingdom was about to run out: the Mark Trade was
  the last century of a fire that needed more than any birth-rate could give.
  The Spire was the panic, not the plan.
- **Now**: the Ember is a winter or two from the Spire. The dragon is a season
  from waking. The Ashland column is on the stair. No ship has come in twenty
  years and the Abbey thinks the first one will be soon.
- **After the climb**, whichever ending: the seasons turn or they do not; the
  unwritten are read or they are not; and one Forsaken has stood in the
  hearth-room where nobody unmarked was ever meant to stand. Every ending is a
  new founding. The bible's open questions (LORE §10) are where the next
  kingdom's first betrayal will come from.
