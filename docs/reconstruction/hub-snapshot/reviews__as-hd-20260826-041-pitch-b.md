Direction B — The Manufactured Dawn
In a world where dawn must be manufactured, the dying Ashen Spire burns discarded histories into daylight, and each shorter morning means fewer possible lives remain. Ressa Keld, a route-scribe recorded as both dead and living, climbs through a feud between the Ember Ledger clans and the Margin clans to decide whether continuity is worth the erasures that sustain it.

Discarded history is burned into daylight

PITCH / NOT CANONDirection in four parts
World, character, Spire, and item-lore treatment
01World pressure
Every settlement surrenders one communal memory at dusk so the Spire can make morning. The days are shortening, while erased versions of roads, homes, and kin return as mutually contradictory evidence.
PITCH / NOT CANON
02Character, clans, and motives
Ressa wants to learn which history made her impossible. The Ember Ledger clans would ration erasure to preserve one navigable present; the Margin clans would release every rejected history; Ressa seeks a third outcome in which no authority may silently erase a life.
PITCH / NOT CANON
03Why the Spire / discovery structure
The Spire is a civic instrument built to compress incompatible histories into one stable world, not a throne or divine monument. Its ascent moves through successively older record chambers, letting inscriptions, altered architecture, and disputed inventories reveal the original public bargain and its later capture.
PITCH / NOT CANON
04Item lore / player-facing tone
Lore-bearing objects use up to three terse voices—a maker’s claim, a later bearer’s correction, and the Spire’s omission—so discovery comes through disagreement rather than exposition. The tone is mournful, investigative, and morally exacting, with human choices kept clearer than the metaphysics.
