Direction A
The Goldbough’s inward collapse did not merely raise the Ashen Spire; it drew every promise left unfinished in the shattered kingdom into the climb, where these “remainders” cling to tools, doors, and names. Ossia Marn, a promise-reader of the pitch-only Remainder Kin, ascends to learn whether the Sovereign Ember should return those burdens to their makers, release the living from them, or reveal which promises were invented to rule.

PITCH / NOT CANONConcept map
Promises become burdens people can carry
Unfinished promises collect in ordinary objects. The climb asks who should inherit them, who may refuse them, and which promises were invented to control others.

PITCH / NOT CANONDirection in four parts
World, character, Spire, and item-lore treatment
01World premise / pressure
As ember flows upward, unfinished promises are pulled from settlements and fixed into ordinary objects. Roads, workshops, and households still function, but their people increasingly inherit duties without knowing who first chose them—or whether anyone did.
PITCH / NOT CANON
02Characters, clan, and competing motives
Ossia Marn discovers that one promise attributed to her dead mother exists in two contradictory forms. The Remainder Kin’s elder witnesses want every burden restored exactly to preserve social continuity; Ossia’s younger circle wants each living bearer allowed to refuse inherited obligations; dispossessed outsiders want the records opened so fabricated claims can be exposed.
PITCH / NOT CANON
03Why the Spire / discovery structure
This direction treats the Spire as a pressure vessel and arbiter formed by the Goldbough’s collapse: each height gathers older and more consequential remainders, while the crown concentrates the unresolved question around the Sovereign Ember. The history is discovered through locations, object custody, brief encounters, and contradictions—not a required exposition chain.
PITCH / NOT CANON
04Item-lore approach / player-facing tone
Equipment and keepsakes carry two restrained fragments: a first claim describing what someone promised the object would do, and a last use showing what a later bearer actually asked of it. Their gap reveals character and clan history without asserting an item behavior, reward, status, or combat outcome.

PITCH / NOT CANONDistinguishing strength
One compact narrative grammar joins the upward-flowing ember, the purpose of the Spire, competing human motives, and item-scale discovery. It can make ordinary carried objects feel consequential while keeping the documented narrative delivery ambient and economical.
PITCH / NOT CANONRisk
The promise/remainder metaphor could become abstract, repetitive, or morally one-note, and “truth stored in objects” could be mistaken for a proposed mechanic. Any selected development must vary the kinds of obligation, include sincere and harmful promises on every side, and keep all behavior-facing implications behind Game Systems and Product Owner validation.
