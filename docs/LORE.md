# AshenSpire — Lore Bible

The one-line premise, expanded into a world the game's existing content already
points at. Everything here is consistent with `docs/GDD.md` §4, the enemy roster,
the event pool, the relic list, the four classes and the three citadel paintings
in `art/title-background-variations/`. Nothing here is required reading for a
rule (GDD §4.3): it is the ground the content stands on, not a script the
player must follow.

**Premise, one breath.** You are a Forsaken. The old kingdom was three great
cities, and at the heart of each city stood a citadel tower, and at the top of
each tower burned a flame. The flames are cold. The cities are ash. The only
heat left in the world is inside the corrupted, as cinders. You climb the three
towers, you kill what holds the heat, you take the cinders, and you relight the
flames — or you decide the old glory is not worth the price and take the crown
for yourself.

---

## 1. The Three Seats

There was no wilderness in the old kingdom. There were three cities, each large
enough to fill a valley, joined by viaducts and river roads, and each built
around one **citadel tower**. The towers were not defences. They were hearths.
Each held one of the **Three Flames**, lit from the **Sovereign Ember** — a
coal of fallen starstone that gave heat without ash — and the flames ran the
kingdom.

| Seat | The tower | The flame | What it kept | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Bastion** — a fortified ridge city, stepped walls, heavy stone | [Ashen Bastion](../art/title-background-variations/ashen-bastion.webp) | **The Field Flame** | The land. Harvest, healing, the seasons turning on time. Its bell told the marches the hour. | Act I — the Fallow Marches |
| **The Citadel** — an island tower in a river city, bridges and gilded water | [River Citadel](../art/title-background-variations/river-citadel.webp) | **The Court Flame** | The law. Oaths held, a knight's word binding, the throne answered to. | Act II — the Stitched Court |
| **The Observatory** — a spire of astronomical stone above misty terraces | [Forgotten Observatory](../art/title-background-variations/forgotten-observatory.webp) | **The Crown Flame** | The dead. Names written and kept, and the Sovereign Ember itself, fed in the crown. | Act III — the Ashen Crown |

The kingdom called itself the **Goldbough**, after its crest: a gilded branch,
three flames on three twigs. The name survives on relics (Goldbough Sapling,
Golden Sprout, Goldleaf Charm) and on the Goldbough Avatar, a heraldic idol
grown over by a sapling planted as the crest. There was never a great tree.
There was a coat of arms, and a kingdom that believed in it.

Each flame had an order: **Wardens** in the Bastion, **Knights** in the Citadel,
**Saints** in the Observatory's Furnace Chapel. Each order kept a horn, a bell
or a censer. Several survive as relics (Warden Horn, Ancestral Horn, Howling
Standard, Radiant Aegis) and none of them work the way they used to.

## 2. The Burning

Nobody living agrees on why the flames went out. The three tellings the player
meets on the road are all partly true:

- **The Warden's telling.** The Crown Flame was fed too much. The Saints,
  wanting the dead to never be forgotten, burned cinders in the Chapel until the
  Ember could not hold the heat, and the Observatory caught from the top down.
  The other two towers followed, because the flames were one fire.
- **The Court's telling.** The Wardens let the Blight in. Rot in the marches,
  ignored for a season too long, until it reached the Bastion's bell and the
  Field Flame went out with a sound nobody has been able to describe.
- **The Astronomer's telling.** Starstone falls, burns, and goes out. The Ember
  was never meant to be kept for a thousand years. What happened was not a
  disaster. It was the star finishing.

What is not disputed: the light collapsed *inward*. The flames did not spread
out over the cities; they drew in, and the cities burned toward their towers
until three valleys of stone stood in their own ash. The towers survived,
because the towers were built to hold fire. The Forsaken call the whole road —
three ash cities, three cold hearths, one climb — the **Ashen Spire**, and the
Observatory, tallest and last, is the Spire proper.

Ember still moves in the ruins. Not out from the hearths, as it should, but
*up*: thin, starving, drawn along the old viaducts toward the Observatory's
crown, as if the Sovereign Ember is trying to leave.

## 3. Cinders, the Corrupted, and what "relight" means

When the flames went out the heat did not vanish. It went into whatever was
closest and would hold it. That is the **Blight**: not a plague, but ember
without a hearth, burning inside things that were never built to burn.

- A soldier who held the Bastion's Fell Courtyard gate is still holding it. He
  is a **Wandering Soldier** now, and there is a cinder where his heart was.
- The river court did not want to die, so it had its surgeons stitch it back
  together. The **Stitched King** is what a throne looks like when it refuses
  to be empty.
- The Saints of the Furnace Chapel kept feeding the Crown Flame long after the
  city was gone. The **Furnace Saint** is the one who never stopped.

A **cinder** is the flame's heat in a form that can be carried. Every corrupted
thing you cut down leaves one, and cinders are the only currency in the Spire
because they are the only thing in the Spire that is still alive. The merchant
takes them. The smith burns them. The shrines drink them. And the three cold
hearths will take them too, if you bring enough.

**Relighting a flame** is therefore not a ritual. It is a butcher's ledger. To
put the Field Flame back in the Bastion you have to take back the heat that
went into the Bell Keeper, the Thorn Matriarch, the Fell Warden and every
blight-hound between. The act boss holds the largest cinder in the city; killing
it is what lights the hearth. This is the loop the player already runs — three
acts, three bosses, cinders spent on the way — given its name.

## 4. The Forsaken

A Forsaken is someone the flames *left*. In the old kingdom every soul was
warmed by one of the three — field-born, court-born, crown-born — and was
written into the Chapel's memory at death. The Forsaken were never written.
They lived outside the walls, in the marches between cities, on the far banks:
exiles, the unnamed, the ones the light did not reach. That is why they can
climb. The Blight takes hold through the flame-mark, and the Forsaken never had
one.

The Forsaken Medallion is a slug of cold iron, blank on both faces. It is the
only badge of the order, and it is a badge only in the sense that a gravestone
with no name is a badge.

A Forsaken has no *right* to the Ember. They are not restoring their own
kingdom. Every ending of the climb is a choice about what the glory of somebody
else's civilization is worth:

- **Restore.** Relight all three, return the Sovereign Ember to the Observatory's
  crown, let the cities be rebuilt — and their light will not reach you this
  time either.
- **Claim.** Take the Ember for yourself. Become the hearth.
- **Transform.** Let it out. The ember has been climbing for a reason.

The game does not tell the player which is correct. The Blighted Valkyrie, the
Furnace Saint and the Hollow Astronomer are each the champion of one answer.

## 5. The four who climb

The classes are not professions. They are four different relationships with the
fire.

- **Reaver** — *blood and steel.* Marches-born, a sellsword the Bastion hired
  and never sworn. Fights in two stances because the Wardens taught two: one to
  break, one to hold. Bleed is the Reaver's argument that a thing with a cinder
  in it still has blood to lose. Bloodflame Stance is the closest a Forsaken can
  come to carrying a flame: it burns the wielder first.
- **Rogue** — *ash and opportunism.* A river-city scavenger, one of the people
  the Court stitched over. Learned poison from the Court Surgeons and speed
  from the Marionettes. Does not believe in the flames and does not need to; a
  clean opening is a clean opening.
- **Starseer** — *starstone.* An apprentice of the Observatory who read the
  Astronomer's charts and left before the eclipse. Casts in sequence because
  starstone answers rhythm, not force; the second spell is always the true one.
  Fragile because starstone remembers what it was before it was a hearth.
- **Herald** — *gold and rot.* The last novice of the Furnace Chapel. Spends
  their own health to act because that is what a Saint does: they feed the
  flame from themselves. Their rot is the Blight, turned outward on purpose.
  The Herald is the only climber who actually wants the flames back, and the
  one the flames would cost the most.

## 6. The three cities

### Act I — The Fallow Marches and the Bastion (the Field Flame)

The lowest seat: a fortress city on a ridge, stepped walls, a bell tower at the
crown, the marches it fed spread out below. Umber, iron, faint gold. Growth
without a Field Flame does not stop; it goes wrong. Briar Hermits root
themselves in the outer wards to stay out of the wind. Lantern Moths carry
sparks of ember dust up the walls looking for a hearth. The Goldbough Avatar
kneels in a courtyard, grown into the roots of the crest's sapling, holding out
a hand — it does not know the kingdom is gone.

- **The Fell Warden** kept the Fell Courtyard gate. He still does. He fights
  with a cane because his leg went the way of the marches.
- **The Bell Keeper** rang the Bellfoundry every dawn so the fields knew the
  hour. The bell is cracked and the dawn does not come, and he rings it anyway.
- **The Thorn Matriarch** was the Wardens' last planting: a warden grafted to
  briar to hold the Briar Sanctum after the order failed. She held.

The Grave of the Nameless is outside the Bastion's walls: a cairn of broken
swords, the graves of Forsaken who climbed before you and were never written
into memory. What the player does there follows them up the Spire.

### Act II — The Stitched Court and the Citadel (the Court Flame)

The middle seat: a tower on an island, a city on both banks, bridges and
viaducts and a river that used to carry the tower's light in gold. Gilded
stone, oxidized metal, surgical joins, verdigris. When the Court Flame went out
every oath in the kingdom came due at once, and the Court answered the only way
it could: it had its surgeons stitch every knight, hound and courtier back to
their post. Order, kept by force, past the point of meaning anything.

- **The Stitched King** has a thousand hands because everyone who swore to him
  is still sewn to the Stitched Throne.
- **The Glass Regent** ruled in the Hall of Mirrors while the King was
  "indisposed." She is made of the court's reflection in its own river, and she
  shatters the same way.
- **The Marrow Organist** plays the Ossuary Organ Hall under the bridges. The
  pipes are the court's dead. He is the only one in the Citadel who still
  remembers the funeral service, and he is playing it for all of them at once.

### Act III — The Ashen Crown and the Observatory (the Crown Flame)

The highest seat: a spire of astronomical stone over terraces the fog has
taken, and the Sovereign Ember's own hearth in its crown. Mineral ash, bone
light, concentrated ember. Here the starving ember pools, and here the things
it is pooling *toward* wait. Ash Revenants reform because the Crown Flame's job
was memory, and it is still trying to remember them into shape. Ember-Starved
Pilgrims came up the viaducts hoping to be warmed.

- **The Furnace Saint** keeps the Crown Flame's hearth in the Furnace Chapel at
  the tower's base. He is still feeding it. Beat him and the hearth is yours.
- **The Hollow Astronomer** watched the Burning from the Eclipse Observatory
  and concluded the star was always going to go out. He wants the Ember to
  *leave*. He will call the heavens down to see it off.
- **The Ashheart Dragon** sleeps in the Ashheart Caldera the tower was built
  over, around the last of the deep heat, the wyrm the Wyrm Aspirants and Wyrm
  Lords were consecrated to. It is the Observatory's oldest tenant and does not
  care what you came for.
- **The Blighted Valkyrie** waits at the crown itself. She was the last soul
  written into the Chapel's memory before it burned — the last one the light
  saved — and the Blight took her hardest of all. She is what *restore* looks
  like from the inside.

## 7. Vocabulary for authors

Use these words consistently in card text, event copy, tooltips and item
descriptions.

| Word | Means | Never means |
|---|---|---|
| **Ember** | the living heat of the Sovereign Ember; the thing that flows up the Spire | fire in general, a status effect |
| **Cinder** | ember in a carried, spendable form; the run's currency; what the corrupted leave | a coin, a small fire |
| **Flame / hearth** | one of the three great fires and the tower-top that seats it; the act's goal | any campfire or rest node |
| **Tower / citadel** | the hearth-tower at the heart of a seat | a dungeon, a generic keep |
| **Seat** | one of the three cities, tower included | a chair, a throne (the throne is in the Citadel) |
| **Blight** | ember without a hearth, burning inside a host; the corruption | disease, poison (the Rogue's poison is not Blight) |
| **Forsaken** | one the light never marked; the player, and those who climbed before | a class name, an insult |
| **Nameless** | Forsaken who died on the climb and were never written into memory | generic undead |
| **Goldbough** | the old kingdom and its crest, a gilded three-flamed branch | a tree, the Spire, the current world |
| **Starstone** | what the Ember fell as; the Starseer's element | generic mana or magic |
| **Written / unwritten** | remembered by the Crown Flame or not | literally literate |

Tone: short, concrete, physical. A sentence about the world should name a
thing you could pick up. Avoid "ancient," "eldritch," "eternal." Prefer *cold,
cracked, stitched, starving, still ringing*.

## 8. What stays open

Deliberately unanswered, so events and quest chains have room:

- Who caught the Ember when it fell, and whether they were Forsaken too.
- Whether the Ember is fleeing the Spire or being *called* up by something above
  the Observatory's crown.
- What the Keeper of the Nameless is walking toward, and whether the second
  cairn is an ending or a door.
- Whether a relit flame stays lit after the climber leaves.
